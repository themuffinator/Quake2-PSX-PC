# Changelog

What has changed, per release, written for someone deciding whether to download
this rather than for someone reviewing the diff.

Entries land under `## [Unreleased]` as work happens. Cutting a release moves
that section into a dated one of its own and empties the queue, and the same
text becomes the body of the GitHub release — so the notes are written by
whoever did the work, not reconstructed afterwards from commit subjects.

Headings are fixed; `scripts/changelog.py categories` prints the list, and
`scripts/changelog.py check` rejects anything else. Empty headings are dropped
when the notes are rendered, so leaving one at `_Nothing yet._` costs nothing.

The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and
the version numbers follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).
While the major version is 0, a minor bump is where behaviour is allowed to
change; see [`docs/RELEASING.md`](docs/RELEASING.md).

## [Unreleased]

### Reconstruction
- Player auto-aim now acquires elevated targets using the retail ray search, aims shots along the selected direction and eases the barrel once per logic tick. The camera stays level, and saved games retain the selected direction and barrel motion.
- **A machinegun guard fires bursts.** `soldier_fire`'s machinegun arm stores `pausetime = level.time + 3 + rand() % 8` when `AI_HOLD_FRAME` is clear (module+0x14E8..+0x1540) and, after the round, holds the frame while `level.time < pausetime` (+0x159C..+0x15CC). M_MoveFrame re-runs a held frame's think, so Attack4's frame 41 fires again every 10 Hz tick: four to eleven rounds a trigger pull. The port had dropped the store and fired one. On BASE2, standing in front of guard #2 for 600 frames, it fired 24 rounds; it now fires 99, and `--trace-cre 2` shows frame 41 held for seven ticks at a time.
- **A dying guard fires where it faces, and a dying machinegun guard empties a burst.** Death1's frames 293 and 296 run `soldier_fire` with flashes 5 and 6, which take `aim = forward` (`addiu v0, s4, -5; sltiu v0, v0, 2` at module+0x1274) — at nobody — and the machinegun arm holds those frames exactly as it holds Attack4's. Every other flash aims at the enemy's eye through `vectoangles`, a fresh `AngleVectors` and id's `crandom() * 1000` / `* 500` spread at 8192 units, all now transcribed. A machinegun guard killed through Death1 on BASE2 fires 18 rounds on its way down.
- **The dying Infantry sweeps its burst up the wall.** Death2's twelve `InfantryMachineGun` frames (155..166) aim along `angles - aimangles[frame - 155]`, id's table stored at module+0x1CE4, and the attack frame 194 leads the enemy by `-819/4096` of its velocity. The aim crosses the shot hook now, so the burst climbs the wall and ends in the ceiling instead of every round landing in the player: on JAIL2, 1 of 12 hit the player and 11 the room.
- **A creature's round is traced.** `monster_fire_bullet` and every pellet of `monster_fire_shotgun` end in `0x8004874C`, the one bullet path: from the posed muzzle along the aim `<< 2` (16384 units), the first box the segment enters before the wall takes it — the player, or any creature but the shooter — and the wall gets the smoke puff when nothing does. The port resolved every creature round as a line-of-sight test and a guaranteed hit on the player. The shotgun's pellets are the engine's own random walk, `((rand() - 16384) << 10) >> 14` added to X and the vertical per pellet and never reset (0x80061F80..0x80061FC4).
- **The muzzle is the gun.** Each module blends two model vertices into a muzzle cache at load (`pose_blend_two_positions`, `0x8006CA2C`: their midpoint when one part owns both) and poses it at fire time (`0x8006C6C8`): the Soldier 228/234, the Infantry 200/205, the Gunner 227/231. The port fired from the creature's origin; it now poses the cache on the creature's last drawn mesh.
- **`AngleVectors` reads pitch and roll.** `0x8005BB58` builds forward, right and up from all three angles; the port read the yaw alone, which is exact for a walking creature and fired the Infantry's whole sweep along the floor. `VectorMA` (`0x8005C460`, products divided toward zero) and `vectoangles` (`0x8005F934`, including its X-aligned quarter-turn quirk) are transcribed beside it.
- **The machinegun's recoil reaches the pad.** Every player fire function ends with `SetShockData(player, id, 4096, NULL)` (`0x80040800`) — the machinegun's at `0x8004CA6C` with pattern 5 — and this port had read that call as an SPU emitter and played nothing. MAIN.SYM names it SetShockData, the slots it fills are four per player at GameWindows+732, and their only reader, ProcessShockData (`0x80040B28`), runs once a field from the VBlank ISR and hands PadSetAct two bytes. Both are here (`src/game/shock.c`), with the sixteen patterns at `0x8009D420` transcribed point for point, and SDL drives the pad's two motors from them while VIBRATION is on (`--vibration` turns it on from the command line; it defaults off, as on the console). A machinegun shot is a 16-tick buzz of the big motor at 1536/4096 and an 8-tick burst of the small one: three fields of 95/255 and two of on, which is what a 300-frame BASE1 run reports for 91 shots (276 and 183 fields). Every other weapon's pattern is wired the same way, the chaingun's only when its spin fired, the hand grenade's on the prime and again on the throw (`0x8004A52C`); the train's two calls (`0x8002C778`, `0x8002C8D8`) rumble every pad within 4096 of it, full inside 2048.
- **A hit, an explosion and a Tank Commander's footstep shake the pad too.** These are the three remaining callers of SetShockData (`0x80040800`). The damage function's tail (`0x8005859C`) rumbles the victim's own pad when a player survives a hit, with the pattern its mod selects from the jump table at `0x800ACE5C`: 13 at 4096 for mod 1, 15 at 4096 for mods 3, 7, 19, 20 and 21, and 16 at 2048 for mods 5, 6 and 18. That covers a creature's bullets, blaster and claws, the crusher, and splash from your own BFG. A 900-frame SECURITY `--demo --watch` run goes from 11 rumble requests to 80. QuakeGenExplosion (`0x8005A778`) ends by rumbling every pad within its size of the blast, full inside half of it (`0x8005AA7C`). The Tank Commander's footstep (`module+0x157C`, its module's import `+0x12C`) does the same at the Tank's own origin with ShockData_Stomp, full inside 2048 and nothing past 8192. It is the only such call in any creature module, so the 31 calls once counted on COMMAND were 31 footsteps.
- **Rockets and grenades leave a fireball.** On the disc the rocket's think and both grenades' call QuakeGenExplosion every time they go off (`0x8004ADF0`; `0x80049F9C`, `0x8004A01C`; `0x8004A8E0`, `0x8004A9B0`). The port only did that for destroyable brushes and the gib dispatcher, so a rocket drew its small particle burst and nothing else. Each one now spawns the Explosion model, with its orange light on the walls around it, and rumbles the pads near it. The half-size fireball the gib arms ask for is still missing.
- **Rockets and grenades go off with a bang.** QuakeGenExplosion plays `wep_grenlx1a` through SoundByCreature (`0x8005AA54`) every time it binds the Explosion model, at the point of the blast, and that is the only explosion sound a rocket or a grenade has: the weapon setup registers the sound a second time (`0x8004DD88`) and nothing reads it back. So every rocket and grenade in the port went off silently. They now bang where they burst, and so do the boss gib arms (Boss2, Flyer, Hover, Strider) that raise the same model. A destroyable brush plays the sound twice on the console. PSXEXPLODE calls QuakeGenExplosion and then plays its own report (`0x8002695C`) without checking whether the model bound, and both start a voice of the same request at the same point in the same frame. The port now does both, so BASE1's two scripted detonations sound four voices where they sounded two. The bang is not cut short when the 400-unit fireball dies. KillModel stops an entity's voice only if the sample loops (`0x800731F0`, flag 0x4), and `wep_grenlx1a` is a 1.34 s one-shot on every bank that carries it. `--report` counts the bangs as `audio.explosion_sounds` and the brush reports as `audio.explosive_reports`.
- **A BUTTON runs its own handler.** `0x80029C28` is not the door's machine: it has no delay and no blocked state, clicks `amb_butn2` once when a positive-speed press reaches its stop (`0x80029E18`), arrives silently on a negative-speed press and returns silently, and never returns at all with a zero wait. The port ran buttons through the door's seven states with every door sound mapped to the click, so a press clicked when it started and again when it came back. The exec's own arithmetic is read too: the sign of `travel` is the speed and the target is its magnitude (`0x8002E118`), the axis is the low two bits of the item's +4 byte (`0x80029F94`) — the same byte the exec tests to negate the target — and a button whose object slot is -1 does nothing (`0x8002E0D0`). JAIL2's console button now slides 60 into its console on Z, silently, where it rose 60 on Y and clicked twice; BASE2's clicks once a press; JAIL4's names no object and is inert.

- **The Tank Commander's guns fire from the gun, at you.** Its module blends model vertices into four muzzle caches at load (module+0x9C4..+0xC00): the chaingun 251/260, the blaster 287/289, and the three rocket tubes lerped in thirds between the blends 307/306 and 304/305 (`((B - A) << 5) / 3`, every divide toward zero). Each fire think poses its cache, turns it by the object's matrix and aims at the enemy's eye (`TankBlaster` module+0xC4C, `TankRocket` +0xDE0), and the chaingun sweeps its yaw by 91 per frame up to 182 and back by 128 from 187 (+0x10F8..+0x1144) at the pitch `vectoangles` gives. The port handed the host the figures and nothing else, so every Tank shot was an instant line-of-sight test from its origin.
- **A creature's bolt and rocket fly.** `monster_fire_blaster` (`0x80062000`) and `monster_fire_rocket` (`0x8006210C`) launch the player's own projectiles with the velocity made from the aim — `aim / 64` for the bolt, flags 11 and mod 6, and `aim * 3/2` for the rocket where the player's launcher doubles — so they trail, burst and splash, and the player is now a target of them; the shooter is credited and never hit. The blaster wrapper plays its report through SoundByCreature (`0x80062088`) and the rocket spawner its launch (`0x8004B26C`). This is the Tank Commander's blaster and rockets and the blaster guard's bolt, all of which used to arrive instantly with nothing on screen. On WASTE4 a Tank's blaster volley is visible leaving its arm.
- **The weapon in your hands runs on the game's tick.** The view-weapon driver (`0x8004EE0C`) has one caller, inside the player think, and it reads the frame's one dt. The port ran it on every display frame instead, so above 25 Hz a gun fired and animated between game ticks. It now runs once per tick with that tick's dt, for every player in a split screen. The body's ATTACK pose follows what the driver returned (`0x8004F6D4`), not a count of shots. A dry click (result 2) poses the body like a shot, an attempt the refire clock refused does not, and a round fired in an earlier substep is cleared by a quieter later one.
- **The railgun stops in the first body.** `AnyoneFireInstantRail` asks the actor query once (`0x80049318`) and deals one DoDamage (`0x80049334`). The beam is still drawn to the wall, but only the nearest body is hurt. The port had the PC game's penetrating rail, for the player's railgun and the Gladiator's, Rider's and Arachner's.
- **A body that cannot be hurt still stops a shot.** Bullets, rails, creature rounds and both lasers now use the same actor query as projectiles (`LineIntersectCreCylinder`, `0x800544EC`). That query skips only the shooter. DoDamage then refuses an immune body, but the shot has still stopped there and shields whatever stands behind it. The player's bullets now draw their blood where the round entered the body, not on the wall behind it.
- **A creature's round knocks the player back and down.** DoDamage forms the knockback against the entity origin (`+0x54`), which sits mid-body. The port measured it from the feet and then rebuilt a level hit point to cancel the error, so every round pushed purely sideways. The round's real entry point is now used: a level shot pushes straight back, and one from above pushes back and down.
- **Drowning starts after twelve seconds and does not stop.** `0x8003D358` takes the underwater ambience arm while air is below 3601 and drowns only once it is past that, at `air >> 10` capped at 7 every 300 ticks. The port had the two arms the other way round. A submerged player lost 0 to 3 points a second for twelve seconds, then nothing however long they stayed under.
- Gibs rebound using the SDK's quantized `SquareRoot0` and `VectorNormal`, the same mover the grenades use, so a 3200 bounce comes back at 1798 rather than 1800. The BFG's beam ends at the middle of its target's hull, `(572 - height) / 2` from the origin (`0x80048D4C`). The secret chime plays from the player's origin rather than their feet.
### Client
- **Launching straight into a level hands the player what the campaign would have by then.** `--map BOSS2` used to start with the blaster and 100 health. The loadout comes from the disc, not a hand-written table. The campaign order is the LOADMAP graph walked from BASE0 (all 28 calls read out). Every earlier level's weapon, ammo, armour, bandolier, pack, power-shield and adrenaline placements at the current skill go through the real pickup, so carry caps and the armour conversion behave as in play. At each level's end half the ammo and armour is kept, as if the rest was spent fighting. Keys, timed powerups and health stay where they were found. The player arrives at full health, holding the gun the console's own preference list picks: the machinegun on BASE3, the chaingun on JAIL5 and the railgun on BOSS2 with 11 slugs and 52 body armour. A restart of that level gives the same loadout again. `--no-loadout` keeps the bare blaster start, and MAGDEMO and the arenas are unaffected.
- **A level started directly begins at its own start point.** `--map` and a new game loaded zone 0 and took the first StartPos listed there. The console writes "Default" into the arrival point (`0x800C8CD0`) and loads that record's zone. COMMAND's Default is in zone 2, and its zone 0 opened on a ledge the player fell from and died within two seconds. JAIL5, POWER2 and WASTE2 were near death by frame 300 for the same reason, and BASE1, WASTE1 and BIGGUN opened at points other than Default. A restart now returns to the point the level was entered by.
- **A disc selector opens when `q2psx` is started without `--disc`.** It used to print its usage and exit, which is what double-clicking `q2psx.exe` did. The selector lists the disc played last — selected, and tagged LAST PLAYED — then the others played before it, any image in `disc/` beside the executable or in the working directory, and every optical drive with whether a disc is in it. Each image is opened and identified as it is listed, so a card says *Quake II (Europe)*, `SLES-01534 - PAL` rather than a file name, and anything that is not the game says so before it is chosen. Browse opens the system's own file picker; an image dropped on the window joins the list; mouse, keyboard and a gamepad all drive it, and it acts on the key's release so a held Enter does not confirm the game's first menu. The game takes over the selector's window rather than opening a second one. The choice is kept in `%APPDATA%\Q2PSX-PC\launcher.cfg` (`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/q2psx-pc/launcher.cfg` elsewhere; `Q2PSX_LAUNCHER_CFG` names another file), along with up to eight recent discs. A headless run without `--disc` still fails with the usage, since nobody is there to answer.
- **The game reads a disc in a real drive.** `--disc E:` on Windows and `--disc /dev/sr0` on Linux open the drive itself: the track table comes from the drive's table of contents and every sector from `READ CD` — SCSI pass-through on Windows with `IOCTL_CDROM_RAW_READ` behind it, `CDROMREADRAW` on Linux — so the XA music and the films, which are Form 2, read as they do from a `.bin`. A drive that will only hand over 2048-byte sectors still plays: every level file is Form 1, and the log says the music and films are lost. Checked against an image mounted as a Windows virtual drive, which is the cooked case; no physical drive was to hand, so the raw path has not been run against one.
- `--shoot-to-kill` holds fire like `--shoot` until a creature dies, then lets go, so a death plays out on camera instead of the body being shot past `gib_health` — which stops its death move, as on the console. The zone-load log names the first creature of each module, and each kind of guard, with its index and position, and `--trace-cre N` logs where that creature's rounds land. `--report` counts creature rounds by what they hit, the rounds fired after death, and the flashes.

### Rendering and audio
- **Creatures have muzzle flashes.** The Soldier's, Infantry's and Gunner's export 1 call `0x80031094` while their object's `+0x10C` sign bit is up — raised by the bullet and shotgun spawners only, dropped every frame at `0x8007EE20` — and it draws a 32x32 additive quad of the chars page (UV 64,208, palette 64) turned by a free-running angle across the creature's X/Y plane, plus a light. The port drew no quad and lit every creature shot of every weapon with the player's machinegun radii; it now raises the creature's own 850..1049 / 1200..1599 from two `rand()`s, and a blaster guard, the Tank Commander and the Arachner flash nothing, as on the console.
- **Every creature round is heard.** `monster_fire_bullet` and `monster_fire_shotgun` play `wep_machgf1b` and `wep_shotgf1b` themselves (`0x80061EAC`, `0x80061F04`), whatever the module played first. The Infantry's twelve-round death burst was silent; a Soldier's round is now two voices of one sample, its module's and the engine's.
- **"You have found a secret." holds for its last frame.** `0x80042B98` tests the centre line's age before `0x80042BB0` adds the frame delta, so the frame that reaches 1200 is still drawn — 120 frames at 30 Hz, where the port drew 119. The line, its `*7` backdrop and the fade were already the console's; the capture is pixel-identical to the last release frame for frame until then. The centre line's own two gates are in too: nothing is put up while the initial-state pass runs (`0x80042E54`), and a body too long for the 63-byte buffer becomes "String too long!" (`0x80042F18`) — no string on the disc is.

- **A model is drawn in the order the console picks.** `CalcSortList` (`0x8006A020`) takes the camera-to-model vector into the model's frame by the transpose of its matrix (`0x8006FF80`) and keys block A's eight painter's orders on the octant of its three signs; block B overrides an octant over a range of animation positions with a stream of its own. The port picked "the order whose start face is nearest", a measured stand-in that was right most of the time and wrong close to the eye: the railgun in hand had a slab from the far side of the gun across its coil, black under neutral light and dark green under WASTE1's. The Soldier's, the Tank Commander's and the hand grenade's block-B overrides are applied with their animation position.
- **The railgun leaves its spiral.** `AnyoneFireInstantRail` (`0x8004917C`) ends in three calls to the offsets spawner: a ring 64 units out round the beam, one turn per 1024-unit segment, thirty particles a segment (fifteen in multiplayer, two turns with three or four windows), turned by the shooter's own matrix, drifting outward on ramp 0 for 16 ticks. Its spawn loop runs `segs & ~1` in steps of two, so the spiral covers about half of a long shot from the muzzle, as on the console. The port drew the impact puff and nothing else.
- **A rocket is a rocket, and a launched grenade is a grenade.** Their spawners allocate a model entity — `Rocket` (`AnyoneFireRocketL`, `0x8004AF7C`) and `Grenade2` (`AnyoneFireGrenadeL`, `0x8004A100`) out of the level's CastList — and the port drew both as the hyperblaster bolt's orange box. They now draw as their models, lit like any entity. A rocket leaves at the pitch of its own flight and the yaw of whoever fired it (`0x8004B1D4..0x8004B24C`), lit with a (127,127,127) back colour (`0x800AE9A8`), and circles that attitude by 64 steps as it flies (`0x8004AD34..0x8004ADBC`); a grenade keeps the three `rand()`s it was launched with. A level whose bank lacks the model shows nothing, as the spawner's hide arm does. The Tank Commander's rockets leave its shoulder as the model too. The BFG ball and the hand grenade still use the box.
- **Rockets, grenades and swings leave trails.** BLUR.C is in: `AddBlurPoints` (`0x8002F238`) samples a short list of a model's vertices in world space and prepends them to one of six records as a new generation, out of a 96-point pool; `ProcessBlurs` (`0x8002F51C`) joins each generation to the newer one beside it with a gouraud, semi-transparent quad per vertex pair, shading the newest at the full colour and each older one a sixth or seventh dimmer, and drops a record its owner has left or that has not been refreshed for its life. The quads carry no texture page, so they blend additively as the world leaves the draw mode, and every caller asks for (32, 32, 32): a faint grey ribbon. A rocket lays two (vertices 15/21 and 38/30, seven frames), a launched grenade one (0/7), a thrown hand grenade one (2/39), and a rocket's vanishes the frame it bursts. The Arachner's `Melee`, the Berserk's three attacks and the Infantry's `Attack2` lay theirs from export 1 over their own windows and vertex lists; the windows are read as positions, block D times five, which is the only reading that puts each inside its own move.
- **The hand grenade and the BFG ball are their models too.** A thrown hand grenade draws as `Grenade3` at the thrower's view pitched down 1280 (`0x8004A6FC..0x8004A770`); the BFG ball as `BFGBlast`, turned by its `Spin` move — the position gains the frame delta each tick and wraps at the move's end (`0x8004B7FC..0x8004B838`). Nothing projectile now draws as the orange box but the hyperblaster's bolt.

### Fixes
- **A rotating switch clicks once per press, not once per tick.** ROTBUTTON's exec reads the angle it is about to write and returns when it is already non-zero (`0x8002BEFC`..`0x8002BF04`), before the `amb_butn2` click and before the hold is reloaded; the port pressed the switch again on every call. 21 of the disc's 59 ROTBUTTON items sit in a record that runs on every tick the player stands in its volume, BASE1's lift switch among them, so standing at one clicked thirty times a second — 45 clicks in 46 ticks at BASE1's, now 1. The release handler (`0x8002C020`) gates on the angle as well, not on a spent hold, so a switch cannot be left latched down. `--report` counts the clicks as `world.rot_clicks`.
- **A Soldier in BASE1 was drawn through the wall between the raised floor and the terrain-and-water room.** Its spawn origin, (-15523, 114, -13554), is one unit under SecondaryCol's floor there (113), so no cell of the eroded hull holds it; the draw looked the cell up afresh every frame, got none, and filed the body by global depth — the one path with no portal behind it — so it was painted over the wall from the raised floor at (-13748, -16758). The console never loses the cell: the creature's link object keeps it at `+0xA2` and the draw area at `+0x9E` (read by the model draw at `0x8006BE9C`), NewTrueMove3d walks the lookup out from the old cell and keeps it on a miss (`0x80045D98`, `0x80045E04`), and UpdateBoxStuff (`0x80054DD4`) rewrites `+0x9E` from it. The client now does the same, and a body that has never stood inside SecondaryCol takes the area of the PrimaryColl cell holding it — the hull UpdateBoxStuff's own mode 2 reads, and one that numbers areas the same way for 10,564 of the 10,571 cells checked across the disc. The blood spray takes the same area, so it still sorts with the body.
- An item that no cell of the loaded zone holds is not drawn. Item groups are not all zoned, so JAIL3's zone 0 places a Large Medi P that stands in zone 4, ten thousand units past zone 0's last cell; with no cell it had no area and went to global depth too. It is the one such item on the disc, and nearer walls happened to cover it from every view in zone 0, so nothing on screen changes; `--shot` runs now report it as `outside the zone`.
- The machinegun's muzzle flash takes two `rand()` draws, one per radius (`0x8004C970`, `0x8004C990`), not one shared draw; and the chaingun's is bigger than the machinegun's — 350..549 and 900..1199 (`0x8004CD48`..`0x8004CD94`) — where the two had shared 250..349 and 700..899.
- A shot writes only the kick fields its fire function stores: pitch for the blaster, shotguns, launchers and railgun, all three for the machinegun, yaw and roll for the chaingun (two draws, both `>>> 11`), and none — with the 30-tick deadline left running — for the hyperblaster, the BFG and the hand grenade.
- The muzzle is pulled back into the room before a shot leaves it. Every fire function except the BFG's and the hand grenade's moves from the shooter's origin to the muzzle through PrimaryColl (Find3dSpaceGivenSecondary, `0x80055054`) and builds the shot from where that stops; the port used the unclipped point, which aiming up under a low ceiling put on the far side of it.
- An empty machinegun leaves its burst loop. A dry return from the frame driver's call sets the view model to IDLE on the spot (`0x8004FFF4`), so the idle pass that switches weapon is reached; the port held the loop for as long as the trigger was down, clicking dry. The chaingun and hyperblaster raise the dry latch instead (`0x80050230`, `0x800502FC`), and the quad's sound follows only a round that fired.
- A mover's sound comes from the centre of its object's own box, plus the live displacement except on a button — a cage lift's collision slabs had moved its sounds off-centre.

- A rail slug reaches 16384 units. The railgun's fire function passes the aim untouched and the rail shifts it (`dir << 2`, `0x80049224`); the port traced the bare aim, so every slug stopped 4096 units out.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-21

### Reconstruction
- **Creatures walk the patrol routes their level authors gave them.** A quarter of the disc's placed creatures — 174 of 651, 9 of BASE1's 22 — name a path corner in their spawn record, and this port had never spawned a corner or installed the resolver that finds one. The console has a dedicated spawner at `0x8007F390`, reached from `0x80057588` where the literal `"PathCorner"` picks the group out by name: it allocates through `0x8005F848`, which stamps class byte 114 and raises the in-use bit, writes the record's `+0x10` into `entity+0x18` as the corner's own targetname and `+0x14` into `entity+0x16` as the next corner's, and copies the origin VERBATIM — no eye lift, no thirty-unit nudge and no drop to the floor, all of which a creature record gets. `monster_start_go` (`0x80061BA4`) then resolves the creature's target with G_PickTarget (`0x8005F708`), sees class 114 at `0x80061C10`, turns the creature at the corner and calls its walk handler; with no resolver every lookup failed and it took the other arm instead — target cleared, pause set to forever, stand. BASE1 zone 1, 600 headless frames: `creatures.moved` 0 before, 5 of the 8 live creatures patrolling after. - **A Soldier is a Soldier-sized target and a Tank Commander is a Tank Commander-sized one.** Every creature in the game was shot at as one 286-radius, 572-tall cylinder, because the port derived that cylinder from the movement hull and `q2_monster_init` gives every creature the same ±286 hull. - **A creature turns the corner instead of walking at one it can never reach.** The port accepted a path corner only when the corner's own origin fell inside the creature's hull, and said in a comment that a corner is a point with no bounds — which is the one thing the console goes out of its way not to do. `BoxEdicts` (Q_SHARED.C `0x8005C7C0`) never reads a candidate's stored bounds: it calls `CalcAbsFromCreBox` (`0x8005CB00`) on the candidate first, and that function's first instruction is `lbu v1, 35(a0)` against 114, the PathCorner class, taking an arm that invents `origin - 96` and `origin + 96` on all three axes (`0x8005CB28`, `0x8005CB58`). The acceptance window is therefore the creature's hull grown by 96 — 382 units per axis, not 286 — and because `VectBoxOverlap` (`0x800552D8`) rejects only on a strict `slt`, a corner exactly 382 away still counts; vertically the port had no slack at all, so a corner authored a little below the floor a creature settles on was simply unreachable. The scan runs every overlapping corner now, too, as `0x8005F5A0`'s jump into the loop's own increment does, because two corners can sit inside one grown hull and stopping at the first would let the wrong one shadow the movetarget. SECURITY zone 0, 400 headless frames: `creatures.shots` and `creatures.pursue_arrived` both read 0 where they were 9 and 10 — a pursuit that cannot advance is exactly that large first number against a near-zero second — and the player is no longer worn down where they stand while the patrol walks into a wall. `monster.c` had recorded the right reading of the corner's `-96`/`+96` cube all along; the tree no longer disagrees with itself.
- **A map walked back into is the map you left, script and all.** Crossing a zone seam inside one map already kept every record's latch; a LOADMAP threw the lot away, so a second visit re-armed every one-shot the player had already spent — CREBATCH ambushes re-summoned, STRING messages replayed, INSECRET volumes and progression gates live again, doors a trigger had disabled back. - **The waste station's pistons stroke out and back for ever instead of sliding out once and sticking.** `PSXPISTON_M` (`0x8002CEB8`, QEVENT.C) is not a door and never was: it is a continuous raised-sine oscillator that `PSXPISTON` (`0x8002E9EC`) installs at `obj+0x2C` and arms with a one-shot latch. This port built a PISTON as an ordinary axis mover and ran it through the generic seven-state machine, so WASTE1 zone 1's three pistons slid out at constant speed, held while the player stood in the volume, and never came back — and they travelled the wrong way, because `0x8002CFD0` negates the target before the multiply and the state machine displaced by plus it. Each tick now advances a phase by `speed * dt`, wraps it at `2 * |target| * 16`, and reads the displacement out of the same 4096-step sine table the rotators use: `((sin + 4096) * -target) / 8192`, truncated toward zero by the `bgez; addiu 8191; srl 13` idiom, so the slab eases out to *minus* target and back. `item[+16]` is not a hold time — it is `obj+0x4E`, a half-stroke count decremented once per end-of-travel crossing (`0x8002CFF0`), and zero means for ever, because the `beq v0, zero` at `0x8002CFF8` skips the counter entirely. A trigger that arrives while a piston is stroking is now a no-op, which is what `0x8002EA2C` tests before it writes anything, so a player standing in the volume no longer restarts it every tick. The `break 0x1C00` guarding the divide at `0x8002CF80` is an assertion, not a survivable case; no PISTON on the disc authors a zero travel, and the port stands still rather than dividing.
- **The gun in your hands carries the barrel pitch the console gives it, and the "that term is provably zero" note is retracted.** `0x8004F3E4`..`0x8004F3F8` reads the view weapon's interpolated clip rotation X, adds the owner's `client+0x46` to it and hands the sum straight to `RotMatrixYXZ` (`0x80089E38`) — one pitch term the camera's own matrix never sees, which is why the barrel moves where the view does not. - **`q2psx-inspect syms` reads the linker's own symbol table, which turns "has anyone looked at this code?" from a judgement into a lookup.** The North American disc, `SLUS-00757`, ships `/MAIN.SYM` — 3,495,896 bytes of Psy-Q `MND` v1, left in the build by whoever cut the master — and no other catalogued build carries it; pointed at the PAL disc the command says so and stops. The format is a flat stream of records, each a `u32` address, a `u8` type and a payload whose length the type decides, with no index and no record length, so a type whose payload size is guessed wrong does not produce one bad record: it desynchronises everything after it, and the damage looks like data rather than like a parse error. The reader therefore stops dead on a type it does not know rather than resynchronising, and the check that the whole thing is being read correctly at all is that the last record ends on the last byte. It does — the walk accounts for all 3,495,896 bytes over 167,089 records — and what comes out is 1,037 functions with the source file and first line of each, grouped by the 75 C files they came from (`QFR_SING.C` 65, `EVENT.C` 57, `QEVENT.C` 56, `MODELS.C` 53), plus 2,892 symbols of which 1,829 sit past the last named function: the Psy-Q library entry points, and then the globals — `ScreenYOff`, `CreColList`, the tables this project has been reading by address since before it could name them — with the 850 in the BSS marked, since the table gives its start as `_bss_org`. Addresses are translated into `SLES-01534`'s space through the relocation the two builds already share, so `syms <disc> T_Damage` answers `0x800627F8`, `G_COMBAT.C` line 472, with the USA build's own `0x800627F4` in brackets. The point of it is subtraction: `funcs` can only find a function that is the target of a `jal`, and set against the table's own list that sweep misses 274 of them — 54 in `QEVENT.C`, 33 in `EVENT.C`, 31 in `QUAKEMIS.C`, 30 in `QFR_SING.C` — including 79 of the 85 `PSX*` event handlers, because the script reaches its handlers through a table rather than by calling them. Sweeping this tree for the addresses it cites and subtracting them from the ones the table names is how the gaps this release closes were found.

### Fixes
- Saves now retain live weapon animation and pending shots, projectile flags and BFG beam timing, script scheduler deadlines and WAIT counters, shared random state, creature AI, difficulty, rotators, explosive-group state and visited-map script history. Optional chunks preserve compatibility with older saves; the disc regression checks restored pixels and subsequent simulation state.
- Timer deadlines now use the original strict comparison and rearm through the shared BIOS-compatible random stream, including range-zero timers. Duplicate requests and full timer tables consume no draw. This supersedes the earlier timer rearm limitation recorded above.
- **Every gun-carrying creature fired up to ten times too often.** `M_CheckAttack`'s post-shot reload is `level.time + random() * 20` TICKS: `0x8005DC50`..`0x8005DC58` build `rand()*5*4`, `0x8005DC5C`..`0x8005DC7C` divide by 32767 with the 0x80010003 magic-number sequence, and `0x8005DC88` stores the sum into `entity+0x110`. That is up to two seconds on the ten-Hz clock. The port had the multiplier left at id's seconds value of 2, so the cooldown was 0 to 0.2 s and a Soldier could fire again on the AI tick after the one it fired on. SECURITY zone 2, 3000 headless frames with `--god`: 132 creature shots before, 83 with this one constant corrected.
- **The breadcrumb trail's head pointed at the newest crumb instead of the oldest.** `PlayerTrail_Add` (`0x80060A70`) writes AT the head — `0x80060AEC` loads it, `0x80060B04`/`0x80060B14`/`0x80060B24`/`0x80060B38` store into that slot — and only afterwards, at `0x80060B88`..`0x80060BA4`, reloads it, adds one and masks with 7. So the head names the slot about to be OVERWRITTEN, which is the oldest, and that is where PickFirst's scan starts (`0x80060BF4`). PlayerTrail_LastSpot (`0x80060FFC`) says the same thing from the other side: the most recent crumb is `trail[(head - 1) & 7]`. This port advanced first and wrote at the new head, so both picks always answered the crumb the player had dropped a third of a second ago and a creature that lost you walked straight at where you are now, through geometry, instead of following the route you took. The crumb's stored yaw goes with it: `0x80060B3C`..`0x80060B80` subtract the PREVIOUS crumb's origin and hand that to vectoyaw, so it is the direction the player was travelling and not the way they were looking. And the `valid` byte the port had added is gone — PlayerTrail_Init (`0x80060F20`) memsets all eight twenty-byte slots and raises the active flag, so an unvisited slot is a crumb at the world origin with timestamp 0 and the scan walks past it on timestamp alone, where the guard made pick_next answer nothing where the original answers a stale spot.
- A crumb is laid when the player can no longer SEE the previous one, not on a timer. PlayerTrail_Add's only caller (`0x8007E32C`) first asks for the last spot at `0x8007E2C4`, tests it with `visible` at `0x8007E2D0`, and at `0x8007E2D8` skips the add when it is still in sight. That is what makes eight slots span a whole route: they are corner markers. Dropping one unconditionally every ten frames made them 2.7 seconds of dense samples, so even a correctly ordered trail only ever led a creature back along the last few strides.
- **The crumbs were recorded at the player's eye and consumed as entity origins.** ai_run's arrival test is `dist*dist >= |pos - last_sighting|²` with `dist` one frame's step — about 144 to 230 for a Soldier's run frames — and a crumb in the eye frame carries a constant `Q2_VIEW_STAND - Q2_EYE_BASE` = 290 in Y. 290² is 84100, so the waypoint could never be reached, `AI_PURSUE_NEXT` was never raised again and the pursuit pressed against its first crumb for the whole 25-second search. ### Tools
- `q2psx-inspect` identifies a disc, verifies every level file against the documented schema, and checks every format claim in the docs against the disc — so "we understand this format" is something the build evaluates rather than something a document asserts. It also renders levels, models, HUDs and menus to a PPM with no window, and carries a PS-X EXE loader and an R3000A disassembler for the questions only the executable can answer.
- `stx2avi` demuxes a film into the raw video and audio streams ffmpeg can mux, driving the same decoder the game uses rather than a second one.

### Fixes
- The repository could not be cloned on Windows at all. A tracked file was called `nul.ppm`, and `NUL` is a DOS device name, so Git refuses to create it: the *checkout* failed, not the build. Renamed to `parity-frame.ppm`, content unchanged, and `scripts/check_paths.py` now fails CI on any tracked path Windows cannot create.
- Nothing had ever built on Linux. The link failed on undefined `sin` and `sqrt` because nothing asked for libm, which glibc keeps in a library of its own where Windows and macOS fold it into the C runtime.
- `disc.c` did not compile under GCC either. `fseeko`, `ftello`, `off_t` and `strtok_r` are POSIX rather than C11, and this project compiles as strict C11, under which glibc hides them — and an implicitly declared `strtok_r` would have truncated its returned pointer to 32 bits on a 64-bit host. A CD image can exceed what C11's `long`-based `fseek` reaches, so the POSIX interfaces are declared rather than given up.

### Build and packaging
- The version lives in one file, `VERSION`, which CMake reads to seed `project()` and the generated `version.h`. A binary, a tag and an archive cannot disagree about what was built.
- A manual release workflow builds Windows, Linux and macOS, tests each, and only then tags and publishes — so a tag never points at a commit that does not compile. `scripts/` holds the version, changelog, release-note and packaging tools it runs, each usable by hand.
- CI builds and tests on GCC, Clang, MSVC and Apple Clang with warnings as errors, and checks that the version each binary reports is the one in `VERSION`.
- A fetched SDL3 never shipped in the Linux or macOS archives, so those would have contained a client that could not start. Shared libraries now build beside the executables and the client carries an `$ORIGIN` rpath.
